Circuit Builder is a breadboard puzzle game where every part behaves like the real thing. No shortcuts, no fail states — just resistors, LEDs, transistors, and eventually the logic gates and memory circuits they add up to.

The curriculum

Four chapters, one continuous arc

Each level teaches one real idea and hands it straight to the next one. By Chapter 4 you're not doing anything conceptually different from Chapter 1 — you're just doing more of it at once.

CH.01

Basics

Complete a loop, and an LED lights up. Break it anywhere in a series circuit and both LEDs go dark; wire the same two LEDs in parallel instead and each one keeps working independently of the other.

Control & Sensing

Resistance stops being fixed: a potentiometer puts it under your thumb, a photoresistor puts it at the mercy of the light in the room. Then the transistor arrives — a small control current switching a much larger one, the one building block every gate in Chapter 3 is made from.

CH.03

Logic Foundations

Every gate here is built from the same discrete transistors and resistors you already know, wired into AND, OR, NAND, NOR, and finally XOR — reading two inputs and deciding a single true-or-false answer.

Digital Memory

Two cross-coupled gates that hold a value with nothing but their own feedback loop. From there: a latch that only listens while enabled, a flip-flop that toggles once per pulse, and a half adder that actually adds two bits together.

How it teaches

Built to be relaxing first, correct second

No fail state

Wire it wrong and nothing happens except the circuit staying dark. There's no timer, no lives, no penalty for trying something and watching it not work.

A hint that never runs out

Stuck is never the end of the road — the hint button walks you to the exact next wire, every time, as many times as you need it.

A real schematic, always on hand

Pull down the Blueprint sheet any time to see the standard schematic symbol for what you just built, next to the breadboard version you actually wired.
